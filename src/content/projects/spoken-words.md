concept: |
  The performer repeatedly says "spoken words." A machine listens to their voice
  and returns its pitch and movement as synthesized sound. Each response becomes
  denser, until imitation develops into an exchange that neither
  side fully controls.

  Contemporary algorithms often act like mirrors, learning our expressions and
  returning amplified versions of them. Spoken Words stays with that mirror. The
  performer keeps answering their imitation until the exchange becomes an argument,
  a collision, and eventually something close to a dance.

technical:
  - "Built in Max/MSP; each utterance is analyzed for pitch, loudness, duration, and spectral features."
  - "A JavaScript state machine learns recurring patterns from what it hears."
  - "Those patterns drive three banks of physically modeled accordion reeds."
  - "Both voices are encoded in third-order Ambisonics."
  - "When they overlap, their sound fields approach, meet, and exchange positions across the loudspeaker array."

Spoken Words began as word play: repeating a phrase until it loses its meaning, a game familiar to most people. Returning to it alone, the voice starts to sound like something that asks for an answer.

That reversal pointed to imitation as a structure running through many media, where a machine learns what we say and returns it slightly changed. Reading around the idea suggested it could carry a performance, and the work took shape with a machine as the other side of the exchange.
